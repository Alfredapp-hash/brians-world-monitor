The NCI Engineered Reality Score

A transparent, structured rubric for measuring signals of coordinated media manipulation — indicator by indicator, in the open.

What this is — and what it is not. The NCI scale measures indicators of coordinated manipulation. A high score means many manipulation techniques are present in how a story is being told across outlets. It does not, by itself, prove that a deliberate influence campaign exists, that any claim in the story is false, or that any specific actor is responsible. It is a structured prompt for skepticism, not a verdict.

Every story that appears across two or more outlets is scored against 20 indicators, each rated 1 (not present) to 5 (strongly present). The raw total (20–100) is normalized to a 0–100 scale and mapped to five bands.

About 14 of the 20 indicators are computed automatically from the live coverage itself — publication timing, phrase synchronization across outlets, and direction-neutral language lexicons. The rest require world knowledge (who benefits, what context is missing, whether the statistics are cherry-picked, historical parallels) and are scored on demand by a local AI model or by you directly. Any indicator can be overridden with your own judgment; the scale was designed for a human in the loop.

The five bands

0–20Low indication of coordinated manipulation
21–40Some persuasive or propaganda techniques present
41–60Significant manipulation indicators
61–80Strong likelihood of an organized influence campaign
81–100Extreme, highly coordinated engineered-reality environment

Most ordinary news scores low. Higher bands are reserved for coverage showing many, specific, citable manipulation techniques at once.

The 20 indicators

Each is scored 1–5. "Auto-scored" indicators are computed from the live coverage; "AI / manual" indicators need world knowledge and are scored on demand.

Story bursts across many outlets nearly simultaneously, or lands at a strategically convenient moment.

How the automatic scoring works

Synchronized phrasing is the core signal. When multiple outlets use the same distinctive multi-word phrase, that is either wire-service copy (normal, and labeled as such when a wire agency is among them) or coordinated messaging (flagged when no wire source is behind it). A phrase repeated across many outlets, or pushed for days, weighs heavily.

Timing looks at how tightly a story bursts across outlets. Emotional and framing language uses direction-neutral lexicons — the same words are flagged whoever uses them, so the score does not take a political side. Coverage asymmetry notes when independent outlets cover something mainstream ignores, or when wall-to-wall coverage has no independent corroboration.

Direction-neutral by design. The language lexicons flag emotive and delegitimizing terms regardless of target. A story is not scored higher for criticizing one side or another — only for how it is told.

Use it as a starting point

A high NCI score is an invitation to look closer, not a conclusion. Read the source columns side by side, check what the local press says, follow the unique claims to their origin, and decide for yourself. The tool's job is to surface the pattern quickly; the judgment stays yours.
